Section 1 · Reading

5Ws and 1H

What it means

Find the six facts that explain an event

Who identifies the people, what identifies the event, when gives the time, where gives the place, why gives the reason, and how explains the method or steps.

Spotting technique

Match the question word to a clue

  • Who: names, roles, groups, or pronouns.
  • When: dates, clock times, days, and sequence words.
  • Where: buildings, rooms, streets, towns, and position words.
Spotting technique

Trace reasons and actions

  • Why: look for because, so, reasons, or goals.
  • How: look for action verbs, steps, and words such as first or then.
  • What: identify the central action or problem before choosing a detail.
Answer strategy

Return to the exact sentence

  1. Read the question word first.
  2. Underline the names, times, places, reason words, and action steps in the story.
  3. Find the paragraph that contains the needed fact.
  4. Choose the answer supported by the passage—not merely an answer that sounds possible.

Section 2 · Grammar

Past-time grammar

Past simple

A completed past action

Lena found a damaged book yesterday.

Past continuous

An action in progress

They were painting when the table moved.

Past perfect

An earlier past event

They had finished before the rain started.

Past perfect continuous

Duration before a past point

They had been working for three hours.

Past sequence

Place events on a timeline

Use before, after, when, and by the time to show which event happened first.

Used to / would

Past habits and states

Used to can describe old habits or states. Would normally describes repeated actions, not past states.

Be used to

Already familiar

Mrs. Bell was used to busy events. The form after to is a noun or an -ing form.

Get used to

Become familiar

Marcus got used to sorting books. This describes a change from unfamiliar to familiar.

Choosing the form

Use the time clue and the event order

  1. Find markers such as yesterday, while, before, and for three hours.
  2. Decide whether the event was completed, in progress, or earlier than another past event.
  3. Check whether the sentence describes a repeated action, an old state, existing familiarity, or the process of becoming familiar.
