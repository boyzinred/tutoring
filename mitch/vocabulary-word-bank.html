Days 1–30 — Learn

Every study day is two separate runs of Exercise 2. Do not put them in one selection: mixing 50 unfamiliar words into 200 familiar ones hides how the new batch actually went.

Run 1, the new batch. Tick today's day only. Question Word, answer Meaning. Leave “Retry a word until it is right” on. 50 words.

Run 2, the review. Tick the days 1, 3, 7 and 16 back and leave today's unticked. Question Meaning, answer Word, because producing the word is the direction that fails first. Set Word limit to 60 so a ~200 word pool becomes a random 60, and put 15 in Timer in minutes.

On day 20 that is: run 1 = day 20; run 2 = days 19, 17, 13 and 4. Early on there is nothing 16 back, so tick only the boxes that exist.

After Day 30 — Consolidate

No new words — the bank stops at day 30. Use Check range to sweep the whole thing in blocks.

Pass one. Word → Meaning, six days a block: 1–6, 7–12, 13–18, 19–24, 25–30. 300 words each, 25 minutes on the timer.

Pass two. Same five blocks, but Meaning → Word. Harder, and the direction speaking and writing need.

Pass three. Question language Example sentence: the word is blanked out of its example and you type it back. This is the only mode that tests the word in a real sentence, so save it for words you already half know.

Rehearsal. Tick All days, Word limit 100, timer 20. A random hundred out of the whole bank is a fair read on where you actually stand.

How the Table Works

The table holds two columns: the word and its meaning. Use Blur Word or Blur Meaning to quiz yourself in place, and click a blurred cell to peek at just that one; the setting is remembered per section.

The + button on a row opens everything else: the part of speech, the study day, the usage note — when and how to use the word, the pattern it takes, the mistake learners usually make — and an example sentence. Open all notes does a whole day at once.

The three stars are yours to set: one for a word you are still learning, three for one you are confident with. Ratings are saved in this browser.

The Daily Mix

Each day carries the same spread, so grammar words, set phrases and interjections are learned alongside content words rather than left out:

Nouns — 15
Verbs — 10
Adjectives — 6
Adverbs — 5
Function words — 6
Fixed expressions — 5
Interjections — 3
Total — 50

A word that belongs to two parts of speech is listed once for each. The usage note is the important part: it gives the pattern or the register rather than repeating the definition.
