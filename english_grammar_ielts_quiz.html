Fundamental grammar

Possessive Nouns

Rule: Add 's to a singular noun (the student's name) and to an irregular plural noun that does not end in -s (children's books). For a regular plural already ending in -s, add only an apostrophe (students' names). For joint ownership, add 's to the final owner: Ella and Ned's grandson.
Fundamental grammar

Using Whose

Whose asks about possession: Whose book is this? Who's is a contraction of who is or who has: Who's taking the picture? They sound the same, so use the sentence meaning to choose the correct spelling.
Fundamental grammar

Possessive Pronouns and Adjectives

Possessive adjectives come before nouns: my pen, her bag, their room. Possessive pronouns stand alone: mine, hers, theirs. Remember: its shows possession, while it's means it is; their shows possession, there indicates place, and they're means they are.
